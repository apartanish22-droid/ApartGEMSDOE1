# Preregistered research queue — not run in this stopped lane

Ranked qualitatively by **hypothesized** potential for DTI improvement versus cost, not by measured or projected leaderboard scores. No new experiment was run: the inherited MAW-GSI lane hit the protocol's mandatory duplicate stop. Novelty means no exact implementation was found in this repository's current `src/apartgems` code; it does not claim novelty across all sibling projects. Every idea requires its own assigned lane, strict surface/final registry checks, feature canaries, and a fold-local comparison against a comparable current holdout best before any selector step.

| 1 | **Signed paired-flank agreement**: detrended elevation and GeoDAWN magnetic anomaly / gravity anomaly from the competition stack, optionally 3DEP DEM. | Detect a persistent, signed elevation step whose paired-flank magnetic or gravity contrast is spatially consistent across multiple windows. A buried basin margin could retain a geophysical contrast even where an unmapped scarp is subtle. **Mimics:** stratigraphic contacts and incised alluvial fans. | Existing MAW-GSI uses multi-azimuth worms and unsigned cross-gradient alignment; a jointly calibrated *signed* flank-step test is not implemented. | Relatively higher hypothesized benefit; medium cost. The input types are specified by [DrivenData](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/), and [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program) describes free DEMs. Locally restored bands are not present now and the mirror has not been authenticated against the organizer. No viability or gain has been validated. |
| 2 | **Flight-line repeatability weighting**: USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line CSVs, elevation and line metadata. | Identify magnetic gradients that persist in overlapping or crossing survey lines after accounting for terrain clearance; stable anomalies in covered basins could point to unmapped structures while suppressing acquisition artifacts. **Mimics:** lithological boundaries and regional magnetic gradients. | The existing code filters gridded magnetic features; it does not perform a repeatability comparison using the original survey line measurements. | Medium potential; high cost. The official [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) lists line CSVs and cautions about terrain clearance. Metadata is publicly readable; the raw files were not obtained or tested from this sandbox, so viability is unverified. |
| 3 | **Depth-normalized paired thermal gradients**: DOE GDR well/spring temperatures and depths, two-metre probes, and structural context. | Contrast nearby temperature-versus-depth observations across candidate edges rather than smoothing all temperature observations into one field. A persistent contrast near a buried structure may identify a hydrologic boundary absent from the mapped surface catalogue. **Mimics:** spatially uneven drilling, groundwater flow, and seasonal temperature effects. | Existing MAW-GSI rasterizes and smooths thermal/probe observations; this proposes uncertainty-aware paired, depth-normalized comparisons. | Uncertain potential; high cost and sparse-data risk. [GDR #1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391) publicly lists the sources and attribution terms, but raw official downloads and field completeness were not checked here. Viability unverified. |
| 4 | **ASTER emissivity contrast along candidate structures**: NASA ASTER GED AG100 V3 thermal-emissivity climatology plus GeoDAWN and DEM. | Look for a spatially coherent thermal-emissivity contrast along independently detected structural edges; alteration and sinter may co-occur with geothermal systems that lack a mapped fault. **Mimics:** quartz-rich dunes, ordinary lithologic variation, albedo and surface-cover effects. | Adds an independent satellite emissivity source; the existing MAW-GSI uses airborne geophysics, terrain, and point thermal records. | Speculative; high cost. [NASA's AG100 V3 catalog](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-ag100-003) describes a 100 m product and open sharing. It was not downloaded, checked for local coverage, aligned, or shown to encode diagnostic sinter; viability unverified. |

The top idea is a future candidate, not an authorization to resume this stopped run. Do not force dots into the few pixels outside a lattice halo simply to manufacture a passing uniqueness statistic; that would be a selection artifact rather than geological evidence.
