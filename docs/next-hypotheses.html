2Flight-line repeatability weighting. Original USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line CSV measurements, line/acquisition metadata, and terrain/elevation context; compare with the released magnetic grid.Separate gradients that persist across overlapping/crossing acquisitions from acquisition-direction or terrain-clearance artifacts. A stable anomaly in an unmapped basin may help prioritize a buried structure. Mimics: lithologic boundaries, regional magnetic gradients, and survey-leveling artifacts.Existing code uses gridded magnetic products and spatial filters; it does not cross-check line-level observations for repeatability by acquisition block or flight direction.Medium relative priority; high cost. The CSVs are listed in the public release, but no raw line file was downloaded or coverage-tested here; do not treat the hypothesis as validated.
3Depth-normalized paired thermal gradients. DOE GDR #1391 “2m Temperature Probes” and “Well and Spring Temperature and Chemistry” datasets, paired by location/depth where metadata permit; use catalogue-independent structural context from the competition grid.Compare local temperature-versus-depth observations across candidate structural boundaries instead of smoothing all thermal points into a single plume. A repeatable gradient break might reveal a blind upflow or groundwater barrier not represented in the fault catalogue. Mimics: uneven drilling/sampling, groundwater flow, seasonal effects, and lithology-controlled conductivity.Existing MAW-GSI rasterizes/smooths thermal and probe measurements. 4ASTER emissivity contrast along independent structural edges. NASA ASTER GED AG100 V3 100 m emissivity/LST climatology, aligned only after independent edge detection; competition DEM/geophysics as context.Test whether spatially coherent emissivity contrasts accompany candidate structures where alteration/sinter may occur, while not treating surface temperature as a direct vent measurement. Mimics: quartz-rich dunes, exposed lithologic units, vegetation/albedo differences, and surface-cover effects.Adds a satellite thermal-emissivity source absent from this repository's airborne geophysics, terrain, and point-temperature feature stack.Lowest/speculative priority; high cost. NASA's catalog says AG100 V3 is global, 100 m, based on clear-sky ASTER observations in 2000–2008, and openly shared. The product was not downloaded or checked for study-area signal/coverage. It is not a 90 m SWIR product and cannot be assumed to map vents.

First action for any future lane

Before any code or raster is touched, obtain a provenance-checked feature stack and authenticated template, rebuild or bind every feature cache to source and content hashes, and synchronize a complete accessible registry. Then preregister exactly one hypothesis and controls, run each feature alone as a leakage canary, and evaluate fold-honest hide-and-recover predictions with only visible-fault-derived context. The current candidate's 99.6787% overlap with one pinned lattice is already sufficient to stop its lane; do not try to place dots into uncovered slivers to game uniqueness.
