1Signed paired-flank agreement: detrended elevation and GeoDAWN magnetic anomaly / gravity anomaly from the competition stack, optionally 3DEP DEM.Detect a persistent, signed elevation step whose paired-flank magnetic or gravity contrast is spatially consistent across multiple windows. A buried basin margin could retain a geophysical contrast even where an unmapped scarp is subtle. Mimics: stratigraphic contacts and incised alluvial fans.Existing MAW-GSI uses multi-azimuth worms and unsigned cross-gradient alignment; a jointly calibrated *signed* flank-step test is not implemented.Relatively higher hypothesized benefit; medium cost. The input types are specified by DrivenData, and USGS 3DEP describes free DEMs. Locally restored bands are not present now and the mirror has not been authenticated against the organizer. No viability or gain has been validated.
2Flight-line repeatability weighting: USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line CSVs, elevation and line metadata.Identify magnetic gradients that persist in overlapping or crossing survey lines after accounting for terrain clearance; stable anomalies in covered basins could point to unmapped structures while suppressing acquisition artifacts. Mimics: lithological boundaries and regional magnetic gradients.The existing code filters gridded magnetic features; it does not perform a repeatability comparison using the original survey line measurements.Medium potential; high cost. The official USGS GeoDAWN release lists line CSVs and cautions about terrain clearance. Metadata is publicly readable; the raw files were not obtained or tested from this sandbox, so viability is unverified.
3Depth-normalized paired thermal gradients: DOE GDR well/spring temperatures and depths, two-metre probes, and structural context.Contrast nearby temperature-versus-depth observations across candidate edges rather than smoothing all temperature observations into one field. A persistent contrast near a buried structure may identify a hydrologic boundary absent from the mapped surface catalogue. Mimics: spatially uneven drilling, groundwater flow, and seasonal temperature effects.Existing MAW-GSI rasterizes and smooths thermal/probe observations; this proposes uncertainty-aware paired, depth-normalized comparisons.Uncertain potential; high cost and sparse-data risk. GDR #1391 publicly lists the sources and attribution terms, but raw official downloads and field completeness were not checked here. Viability unverified.
4ASTER emissivity contrast along candidate structures: NASA ASTER GED AG100 V3 thermal-emissivity climatology plus GeoDAWN and DEM.Look for a spatially coherent thermal-emissivity contrast along independently detected structural edges; alteration and sinter may co-occur with geothermal systems that lack a mapped fault. Mimics: quartz-rich dunes, ordinary lithologic variation, albedo and surface-cover effects.Adds an independent satellite emissivity source; the existing MAW-GSI uses airborne geophysics, terrain, and point thermal records.Speculative; high cost. NASA's AG100 V3 catalog describes a 100 m product and open sharing. It was not downloaded, checked for local coverage, aligned, or shown to encode diagnostic sinter; viability unverified.

The top idea is a future candidate, not an authorization to resume this stopped run. Do not force dots into the few pixels outside a lattice halo simply to manufacture a passing uniqueness statistic; that would be a selection artifact rather than geological evidence.
